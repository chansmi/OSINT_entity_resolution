# Responsible AI Metadata — OpenSanctions Entity Pairs

Answers for the NeurIPS Croissant RAI fields.

## Data Limitations

This dataset is restricted to entity pairs (persons and organizations) drawn from OpenSanctions sources, which over-represent entities relevant to sanctions, politically exposed persons (PEPs), and crime/corruption watchlists. It is therefore not representative of the general population of names. Coverage is strongest for entities with Latin-script names; non-Latin scripts (Arabic, Cyrillic, Chinese, Korean, Japanese) are present but transliteration quality varies across sources. Pair labels were generated by the OpenSanctions deduplication workflow (a mix of automated matchers and human review), so a small amount of label noise is expected. The dataset is class-imbalanced (76.9% positive, 23.1% negative) and the negatives are hard negatives produced by candidate generation, not a uniform sample. It is not recommended for: (a) training or evaluating production sanctions screening systems without an additional held-out screening benchmark, (b) general-purpose name matching outside the watchlist domain, or (c) any safety-critical or high-stakes legal use without independent validation.

## Data Biases

Selection bias: entities are drawn from public sanctions, PEP, and watchlist sources, which heavily over-represent jurisdictions and individuals targeted by Western (US, EU, UK) sanctions regimes and under-represent ordinary individuals and businesses. Geographic and political bias: countries under active sanctions (Russia, Iran, North Korea, Venezuela, Syria, Belarus, Myanmar, etc.) are disproportionately represented relative to their share of global population or commerce. Script and language bias: name variants in Latin script and standard transliterations are better covered than native-script forms, and CJK names in particular have fewer attested aliases per entity. Label bias: positive pairs are skewed toward entities that have already been deduplicated by OpenSanctions reviewers, so confusable cases that survived deduplication may be under-represented among the negatives. Type bias: persons and organizations dominate; vessels, addresses, and other entity types are excluded from this benchmark.

## Personal & Sensitive Information

The dataset contains personal information about real, named individuals and organizations. The following sensitive categories apply:

- Gender (inferred from names in some records)
- Geography (nationality, country of residence, place of birth)
- Language and culture (names in multiple scripts and transliterations)
- Age (dates of birth where published by the source)
- Political or religious beliefs (politically exposed persons, sanctioned political figures)
- Others: alleged criminal involvement, sanctions designations, and law-enforcement watchlist status

All data is sourced from OpenSanctions, which aggregates information that is already publicly published by governments, regulators, and official watchlists. No private or non-public personal data is included, and no contact details, financial account numbers, or government identification numbers beyond what the upstream public sources release are added by this dataset.

## Data Use Cases

The dataset measures entity resolution: given two records describing a person or organization, decide whether they refer to the same real-world entity. It is intended as a research benchmark for evaluating name-matching, record-linkage, and large language model approaches on watchlist-style data.

Validated use cases: zero-shot and few-shot evaluation of LLM-based entity resolution, evaluation of fuzzy and rule-based matchers (e.g. nomenklatura), and prompt optimization research (see accompanying paper). Validation is reported on a stratified test split with standard precision, recall, and F1.

Not validated: production sanctions screening (which uses a different decision threshold and risk model), entity resolution in non-watchlist domains (e.g. customer records, academic authors, product catalogs), fine-tuning of deployed compliance systems, and any clinical, legal, or safety-critical decision making.

## Data Social Impact

Positive impact: provides an open, reproducible benchmark for entity resolution on real watchlist data, which has historically been evaluated only on proprietary datasets inside compliance vendors. Lowers the barrier for academic research on multilingual name matching, hard-negative evaluation, and LLM-based record linkage, and enables fair comparison across methods.

Risks: the dataset names real individuals associated with sanctions, PEP status, or alleged criminal activity; misuse could include scraping for harassment, building shadow watchlists, or making consequential decisions about individuals based on model outputs without human review. Because designations and labels change over time, stale predictions could harm individuals who have been removed from upstream lists.

Mitigations: the dataset is a snapshot of already-public OpenSanctions data and includes only fields the upstream sources release publicly. It is distributed for research use and inherits the upstream OpenSanctions licensing terms (CC BY-NC 4.0 for non-commercial research). Users should consult live OpenSanctions data, not this snapshot, for any operational decision, and should not use model predictions as a substitute for an authoritative compliance workflow.

## Contains Synthetic Data

No, this dataset does not contain synthetic data. All entity records and pair labels are derived from real public-source data aggregated by OpenSanctions.
